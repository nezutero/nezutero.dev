## Who I Am

Administrativaly my name is Dmytro, but I find Dimitri to be better in terms of sound and simplicity
for others. Dimitri is a french first name, its meaning is “devoted to, dedicated to, or follower of
Demeter”, and Demeter is a greek god of the harvest and agriculture. It was really interesting to
find out all that stuff about my name; I wonder if my grandpa who had given me my name was thinking
about it the same way I do. My family name is Nesterenko, which is a Ukrainian surname meaning son
or daughter of Nester/Nestor. Who is Nestor? In Greek mythology it comes from that of Nestor, the
son of Neleus, the King of Pylos and Chloris. I read a bunch of info about it, even thought it was
interesting to learn about my family name's origins, precisely because it is related to me, but
usually I'm not really into it. Ah yeah, and I'm from Ukraine. While I value and appriciate my origins, I consider myself a [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism).

I'm a computer science student, originally from Ukraine, now living in Rennes, France. I wanna master my craft, become a good engineer, build something meaningful, that can change lives of people in a positive/useful manner. Also, I don't believe in limiting myself to a single label.

## Technical Interests

I'm drawn to algorithms and low-level systems; the kind where you think deeply about what you do and why, that require real thinking and understanding.

I value privacy and freedom, which naturally led me to Linux and the free and open source software movement. Understanding how Linux works at a low level fascinates me; that's why I started with Arch Linux before discovering the functional paradigm and declarative bliss of NixOS. The languages that excite me are compiled, efficient ones like C, the greatest language ever created. Languages I'd like to explore deeply in the future include OCaml, Zig, Go, Rust and many more, but these three are the first ones on my list.

Neovim. Truly the best editor ever created, which lets you accomplish everything without touching a mouse. That's why I don't have one.

I use a ThinkPad running NixOS. You can explore more about what I use and my technical interests on my [/uses](/uses) page.

## Philosophy & Growth

Beyond computer science, I'm drawn to philosophy and deliberate living. Nietzsche and Miyamoto Musashi have been particularly influential, along with certain Stoic ideas. I stay open and regularly expose myself to different philosophies. I learn from everything, aiming to become a better human -- stronger, with deeper self-understanding and knowledge of the world. I value deep human connections and find humans fascinating.

I'm an extreme minimalist (I own about <100 items). I like this way of living: it gives me freedom
and forces to appriciate the mode of being, not having. I feel like I have more freedom living this
way. It is also extremely cheap and practical!

I believe in lifelong learning and the need to take on challenges; doing hard things. Every day is an opportunity to grow. Human beings are human beings, not robots. There’s a place for emotions and laziness, being bored and doing nothing; this is important.

I hate the overly capitalist view, which society forces us to take, the focused on productivity misery, very toxic shit. Life is hard; it’s normal to want to rest and do something else. Productivity comes from loving and enjoying your craft, your life, and just knowing that you are doing something cool, something useful to someone -- it doesn't come from capitalistic, toxic aim to gain more profit and optimization of every minute of your life to achive ultimate productive misery. I'm speaking from my own experience here, since I had issues with this, which lead me to mental health issues, misery and burnout.

Human beings have this wonderful desire to grow and improve; every single one of us! And it’s by living in harmony with this drive that we attain true happiness and find meaning in our lives. That’s what I believe.
